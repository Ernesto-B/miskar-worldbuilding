# Solanthis: The Dawn Dominion

**Core identity:** the birthplace of the Celestial Order, the holiest land in Miskar, and the place where faith has become government, culture, law, architecture, military doctrine, and social identity.

Solanthis should feel beautiful, terrifying, orderly, and suffocating.

Not “evil church land.” More like:

> A radiant civilization so convinced it saved the world that it cannot recognize the harm it causes.

That fits your existing Second Age canon really well: the Celestial Order controls magic because the upper hierarchy knows the Seal exists, knows magic weakens it, and believes truth would collapse civilization.

The Celestial Crown having **Emperor of Man vibes** works extremely well, but I would make one key adjustment for D&D:

## The Celestial Crown should be worshipped as “almost divine,” not literally divine

The public should not say, “He is a god.”

They should say things like:

> “He is the Voice Beneath the Sun.”  
> “He is the living witness of the Three.”  
> “He is not a god. He is what remains when a mortal is emptied enough to carry divine will.”

That is creepier, more flexible, and more usable at the table.

It lets fanatics correct outsiders:

> “Do not blaspheme. The Crown is not a god. He is obedience perfected.”

That line tells the party everything.

---

# Regional Promise

When the players hear “Solanthis,” they should expect:

**Sunlit theocracy, fanatical pilgrims, sacred law, forbidden archives, inquisitors, magic licensing, radiant military orders, public confession, divine propaganda, and the uncomfortable feeling that the safest roads in Miskar may also be the least free.**

Solanthis is not grimy. It is clean.

The horror is not darkness.  
The horror is **clarity**.

White stone. Gold banners. Bells at dawn. Public hymns. Confession plazas. Sun mirrors. Processions. Children reciting doctrine. Licensed mages wearing visible brands or sun-sealed collars. Dragonborn watched from rooftops. Tieflings searched at gates. Clerics smiling with total certainty.

---

# The Solanthis Pitch

## What common people say

> “Solanthis is where the sun first answered after the War of Night. No road is safer. No court is cleaner. No priest is wiser. If the world survives another age, it will be because Erin Elios still shines.”

## What skeptics say

> “Solanthis does not burn books. It shelves them where only the obedient may read.”

## What the truth is

Solanthis is the birthplace of the Celestial Order because it was one of the first places after the War of Night where survivors turned terror into doctrine.

The First Age ended with a contradiction: mortals survived because magic returned, but magic also weakens the Seal. The Order’s answer was not to destroy magic, but to **license it, ritualize it, narrate it, and monopolize its moral meaning**.

Solanthis is where that answer became civilization.

---

# The Central Contradiction of Solanthis

Solanthis genuinely protects people.

Its roads are guarded. Its courts are efficient. Its healers are trained. Its granaries are audited. Its monster response is fast. Its clergy can mobilize thousands. Its rituals reduce panic. Its doctrine gives people a reason to endure.

And yet:

Solanthis lies.

It suppresses the Old Gods, edits First Age history, obscures Thaurûn’s nature, controls magic, marginalizes dissenting peoples, and teaches the world that obedience saved Miskar when the truth is far more complicated.

That makes the region much more interesting than “religious bad guys.”

The players should constantly feel:

> “I hate how controlling this place is… but I understand why people trust it.”

# The Celestial Crown

## Public identity

The Celestial Crown is the supreme mortal authority of the Celestial Order.

He is not merely a pope or emperor. He is treated as the living axis between heaven and Miskar.

Titles:

- **The Crown Beneath the Sun**
    
- **The Dawn Made Flesh**
    
- **The Last Witness**
    
- **The Voice of the Three**
    
- **The Unsetting One**
    
- **The Mortal Throne of Heaven**
    
- **The Hand That Holds the Seal**
    

That last one should be restricted. Only high clergy should say it, because it hints at the truth.

## Fanatical belief

The common faithful believe:

> The Crown does not rule because he is powerful.  
> He rules because he has surrendered the right to be wrong.

That is the kind of doctrine that creates terrifying loyalty.

His followers may believe:

- His dreams are omens.
    
- His silence is judgement.
    
- His illness means the world is sinful.
    
- His shadow falling on someone is a blessing.
    
- His blood is never seen because it would be too holy or too dangerous.
    
- He has not looked directly at the sun in decades because “the sun looks through him.”
    
- When he dies, dawn may fail.
    

## DM truth options

I would keep the Crown mysterious for now. Pick one later.

### Option A: Tragic Human

The Crown is just a mortal crushed under divine knowledge. He knows the Seal is weakening. He knows the Order is built on lies. He believes tyranny is the price of survival.

Best for moral complexity.

### Option B: Living Vessel

The Crown is partially inhabited by one of the Great Gods. Not possessed exactly, but overwritten over time.

Best for cosmic horror.

### Option C: Divine Fraud

The Crown has no divine connection anymore, but the institution cannot admit it. The High Cardinals manufacture miracles through controlled magic and relics.

Best for political thriller.

### Option D: Seal-Bound Martyr

The Crown is physically connected to the Seal. His body is a pressure gauge. His pain increases when magic use surges across Miskar.

Best if you want him to be both terrifying and sympathetic.

My recommendation: **Option D with hints of Option B**.

That gives you the Emperor of Man vibe: a revered, half-dead, semi-divine figure whose suffering justifies an empire of fanaticism.

# Solanthis Regional Themes

## 1. Obedience as salvation

People in Solanthis do not think obedience is weakness.

They think obedience is what prevents another War of Night.

Common saying:

> “The undisciplined hand opens the wound.”

Meaning: careless magic, forbidden research, and spiritual rebellion all risk bringing back the Grey.

## 2. Beauty as control

Everything is beautiful on purpose.

Architecture, hymns, military uniforms, public rituals, even executions. Solanthis makes authority aesthetically overwhelming.

A public punishment does not look like a hanging. It looks like a ceremony.

## 3. Light as surveillance

In most fantasy, light means hope.

In Solanthis, light also means being seen.

Mirrors, sun-towers, dawn bells, white streets, open plazas, public confession halls, clerical witnesses.

A rogue feels exposed here.  
A warlock feels hunted.  
A sorcerer feels studied.  
A cleric feels tested.

## 4. Safety with a price

Solanthis should be one of the safest regions for ordinary travel.

That is important.

Players should see peasants who genuinely love the Order because their children are alive because of it.

But the price is compliance.

# Solanthis and Magic

Magic in Solanthis is not banned.

It is **owned by doctrine**.

A licensed mage in Solanthis might have:

- A sun-seal document
    
- A visible badge, ring, brand, or collar
    
- A permitted spell category
    
- A required confession schedule
    
- A spellcasting witness for public work
    
- Restrictions on illusion, enchantment, necromancy, planar magic, and unsanctioned divination
    

Public doctrine:

> “Magic is fire stolen from the wound of heaven. Use it only with clean hands.”

Actual doctrine:

> Magic weakens the Seal, but the population cannot know that directly.

This creates great table tension.

A party caster may be asked:

> “Are you licensed?”  
> “By which branch?”  
> “For field use, civic use, or emergency use?”  
> “Have you submitted your spell list?”  
> “Do you consent to a purity reading?”

# Solanthis and Other Peoples

Based on your racial canon, Solanthis should be especially tense for **dragonborn** and **tieflings**.

Dragonborn have an instinctive resistance to celestial dogma and are associated with Vorkul the Eclipse, which makes the Order deeply suspicious of them.

Tieflings are feared because of their Old God origin, extreme magical aptitude, and connection to volatile or prohibited practices.

So in Solanthis:

## Humans

Most comfortable. Often the majority of clergy, soldiers, bureaucrats, and pilgrims.

## Elves

Respected but watched. Their long memory makes them useful and dangerous.

## Dwarves

Tolerated as practical allies. Their earthbound theology is considered “incomplete but disciplined.”

## Dragonborn

Legally permitted, socially distrusted. Flight near holy sites may be restricted or forbidden.

## Tieflings

Heavily monitored. Often required to register at gates. May be banned from sacred archives or healing districts.

## Drow

Rare and deeply distrusted. Usually treated as spies unless protected by diplomatic status.

## Halflings

Generally welcomed, especially as pilgrims, scribes, cooks, and logistics workers.

## Goliaths

Respected as austere survivors, but seen as spiritually underdeveloped.

# Major Locations of Solanthis

## 1. Erin Elios

**Role:** holy capital of the Celestial Order.

**Identity:** sacred heart of the Order, city of high temples and stricter customs where faith, law, and public life are almost indistinguishable.

**Visual feel:** white-gold city rising above a sunlit coast or high plateau; enormous mirrors redirect dawnlight into temple avenues; bells ring in layered sequences; banners are never dirty.

**Player hook:**

> In Erin Elios, even lies are spoken beautifully.

**What it controls:**

- The Celestial Crown
    
- Highest religious courts
    
- Magic licensing doctrine
    
- Sacred archives
    
- High inquisitorial authority
    
- Canonized history of the War of Night
    

**Active pressure:**

The Crown has begun appearing less often. Publicly, this is framed as holy contemplation. Privately, the High Cardinals are fighting over interpretation, succession, and how much truth must remain buried.

**Adventure uses:**

- The party needs a license, pardon, relic ruling, or forbidden record.
    
- A lower cleric secretly asks them to investigate a miracle that seems staged.
    
- A dragonborn NPC is accused of “eclipse sympathy.”
    
- A Guild envoy disappears after requesting access to First Age documents.
    
- The party witnesses a public ritual where someone in the crowd begins speaking the ancient name **Ghrathûl**.
    

**Raid-style encounter idea: The Mirror Procession**

The party fights during a massive dawn procession while enemies use rotating sun mirrors to blind, reveal, burn, or sanctify zones of the battlefield.

Roles:

- One player rotates or sabotages mirrors.
    
- One protects civilians from stampede.
    
- One identifies the true assassin among identical white-robed penitents.
    
- One interrupts clerics channeling radiant suppression.
    
- One holds off elite Crownwardens.
    
- One tracks which areas are safe from radiant pulses.
    
- One handles the objective: saving, stealing, or exposing the target.

## 2. The Choirless Cathedral

**Role:** forbidden holy site.

**Identity:** a cathedral where song is banned.

**Player hook:**

> The holiest cathedral in Solanthis has not allowed music for three hundred years. Last month, people began hearing singing from inside.

This is an incredible location. It should be one of Solanthis’s best.

**Surface explanation:**

During the early Second Age, a choir of miracle-singers performed a hymn so perfect that it allegedly drew the attention of the Three Great Gods. The choir vanished mid-song. Since then, no singing is permitted inside.

**Actual possibilities:**

Pick later, but here are strong options:

### Option A: The hymn damaged the Seal

The choir accidentally discovered a harmonic pattern that interacts with the Seal. The Order sealed the cathedral not because the miracle failed, but because it worked too well.

### Option B: The choir heard the Old Gods

Their final hymn harmonized with something beneath the divine order. The voices now heard inside are not ghosts. They are echoes from beneath creation.

### Option C: The choir learned Thaurûn’s truth

The final verse named what Thaurûn really was. The Order erased the song and banned music to prevent memory from reconstructing it.

Best fit for your lore: **Option C with Option A underneath**.

**Active pressure:**

The cathedral has begun producing sound again, but only people with weak faith, magical sensitivity, dragonborn blood, or Grey exposure can hear the complete melody.

**Adventure uses:**

- The party must enter without speaking above a whisper.
    
- Sound itself becomes a dungeon mechanic.
    
- Clerics outside insist nothing is happening.
    
- A deaf monk can safely guide them because he reads vibrations in the stone.
    
- The final chamber contains seven empty choir stalls and one occupied seat.
    

**Raid-style encounter idea: The Silent Hymn**

The boss cannot be damaged unless the party manipulates silence, bells, and resonance.

Roles:

- One player rings bells in correct sequence.
    
- One maintains silence zones.
    
- One reads shifting hymn text on the walls.
    
- One protects the bell-ringer.
    
- One interrupts enemies trying to complete the forbidden verse.
    
- One handles adds drawn to sound.
    
- One chooses whether to let the final note play.
    

Consequence: If the party completes the hymn, they learn a major truth, but something in Amon Gûl notices.

## 3. Aurelune

**Role:** pilgrimage and healing city.

**Identity:** the merciful face of Solanthis.

**Visual feel:** moon-white hospice towers, sunlit bathhouses, quiet gardens, rows of pilgrims waiting for sanctioned healing.

The name has “lune” in it, so I would make Aurelune slightly unusual within Solanthis: a city where moonlight, reflection, and gentler celestial imagery matter. It is still loyal to the Order, but softer, older, and more compassionate.

**Player hook:**

> In Aurelune, healing is free. Forgiveness is not.

**What it controls:**

- Pilgrim hospitals
    
- Sanctioned resurrection petitions
    
- Holy water distribution
    
- Confession records
    
- Care for Grey-touched survivors
    
- “Mercy trials” for criminals, heretics, and unlicensed mages
    

**Dark twist: sacred debt**

People healed by Aurelune often owe service to the Order.

Not in a cartoonishly evil way. More like:

> “Your daughter lives because the Dawn heard you. When the Dawn calls, will your house answer?”

That creates generations of obligation.

**Active pressure:**

Aurelune’s healers are seeing wounds that do not respond to divine magic. Some patients cast no shadow at dawn. Others dream of a starless figure retreating into a mountain.

**Adventure uses:**

- A party member or beloved NPC needs rare healing.
    
- The price is service, confession, or surrendering a relic.
    
- A healer secretly believes the illness is not disease but Seal-stress.
    
- Patients are vanishing from the Grey recovery ward.
    
- A tiefling physician has better results than the sanctioned clergy and is about to be arrested.
    

**Raid-style encounter idea: The Ward of Unshadowed Patients**

The party must protect patients while identifying which ones are becoming vectors.

Roles:

- One stabilizes patients.
    
- One tracks shadow behavior as a clue.
    
- One prevents clergy from executing everyone.
    
- One fights manifestations crawling out of reflections.
    
- One keeps healing fountains from being corrupted.
    
- One negotiates with panicked guards.
    
- One decides whether to reveal the truth publicly.

## 4. Varn Keth

**Role:** fortress-city and military shield of Solanthis.

**Identity:** the mailed fist of the Dawn.

**Visual feel:** sandstone walls, gold-edged battlements, sun-forged weaponry, disciplined military streets, execution plazas, barracks-temples.

**Player hook:**

> Varn Keth is where mercy goes to learn formation.

**What it controls:**

- Solanthis military doctrine
    
- Anti-Grey crusader training
    
- Inquisitorial field units
    
- War relic storage
    
- Prisoner transport
    
- Dragonborn and tiefling monitoring in the eastern routes
    

**Active pressure:**

A crusade faction inside Varn Keth believes the Order has become too slow and bureaucratic. They want to force a holy campaign into the Ashenlands, possibly toward Amon Gûl.

This is dangerous because Amon Gûl remains the sealed wound where Thaurûn retreated and the Grey remains densest.

**Adventure uses:**

- The party is hired to train, escort, sabotage, or investigate crusaders.
    
- A commander wants proof the Guild is hiding Grey reports.
    
- A prisoner claims to be one of the Veiled reborn.
    
- Soldiers are being blessed before deployment and returning without fear, pain, or independent thought.
    

**Raid-style encounter idea: The Sunbreaker Drill**

A military trial becomes real when zealots activate an ancient war engine.

Roles:

- One disables radiant artillery.
    
- One commands frightened recruits.
    
- One duels the field-captain.
    
- One moves siege shields to block beams.
    
- One rescues prisoners from execution platforms.
    
- One identifies which “cleric” is actually controlling the engine.
    
- One handles the moral choice: destroy the relic or let Varn Keth keep it for the coming war.

## 5. Dawnwatch

**Role:** observatory, omen-fortress, and celestial surveillance site.

**Identity:** the eye of Solanthis.

**Visual feel:** a high tower or mountain citadel with brass instruments, sun lenses, star charts, chained telescopes, and clerics who map the heavens like military terrain.

**Player hook:**

> Dawnwatch records every sunrise. Three days are missing.

**What it controls:**

- Celestial omens
    
- Star calendars
    
- Grey-pattern tracking
    
- Navigation doctrine
    
- Forbidden astronomical records
    
- Possibly surveillance of magic events across Miskar
    

**Active pressure:**

Dawnwatch has observed an impossible star pattern: a patch of sky behaving like the War of Night never ended.

The High Cardinals want the records sealed. The astronomer-priests are divided.

**Adventure uses:**

- The party needs a star chart to navigate a cursed region.
    
- Dawnwatch can confirm whether a “Starfall walked” rumor is true.
    
- Someone has altered old War of Night records.
    
- The observatory’s lower vault contains pre-Order celestial maps that include symbols of the Old Gods.
    

**Raid-style encounter idea: The Orrery Vault**

A massive mechanical star map becomes the battlefield.

Roles:

- One rotates celestial rings to open paths.
    
- One prevents enemies from aligning a forbidden eclipse.
    
- One reads constellations as combat instructions.
    
- One handles flying or climbing enemies.
    
- One protects the scholar NPC.
    
- One uses light/shadow positioning to weaken the boss.
    
- One chooses whether to preserve or destroy the old sky map.

# Solanthis Factions

## 1. The Celestial Crown

Wants: preserve the Seal, preserve faith, preserve control.

Will do: anything, if the alternative is another War of Night.

Public face: divine father of civilization.

Actual function: living keystone of the Order’s legitimacy.

## 2. The High Cardinals

Wants: manage doctrine, succession, secrets, and institutional survival.

Will do: censorship, assassinations, staged miracles, controlled reforms, quiet purges.

Internal split:

- One wants harsher control.
    
- One wants limited truth.
    
- One wants to weaponize the coming crisis.
    

## 3. The Crownwardens

Elite holy guards of Erin Elios.

Wants: protect the Crown and sacred sites.

Will do: die without hesitation.

Combat style: phalanx discipline, radiant shields, anti-magic formations, body-blocking, coordinated smites.

## 4. The Luminant Inquest

Investigators and mage-licensing enforcers.

Wants: identify illegal magic, heresy, relic trafficking, Guild leaks.

Will do: interrogation, surveillance, forced confession, memory review.

They are not all sadists. The scary ones are calm professionals.

## 5. The Mercy Choir

Healers and confessors centered in Aurelune.

Wants: preserve life and spiritual obedience.

Will do: heal enemies, protect civilians, hide patients, or report dangerous truths depending on the individual.

Great source of sympathetic Order NPCs.

## 6. The Unveiled Dawn

Radical internal sect.

Wants: open crusade, purging of heresy, direct rule over all magic users.

Will do: false-flag Grey incidents, assassinate moderates, provoke war with the Guild.

They are your easiest combat antagonists inside Solanthis.

## 7. The Quiet Eclipse

Forbidden dissent network.

Wants: expose suppressed First Age truths.

Members: lower clergy, dragonborn sympathizers, elven preservationists, Guild contacts, archive scribes.

Will do: smuggle records, hide fugitives, leak relic locations.

They may be good people, reckless people, or manipulated by something worse.

# How Solanthis Should Treat the Adventurer’s Guild

Officially: tolerated.

The Guild is necessary because it handles threats armies, local militias, and the Order cannot reliably handle alone.

Privately: resented.

The Order fears the Guild because the Guild’s “No Hoarding of Threats” principle means dangerous information gets logged and shared instead of controlled by doctrine.

In Solanthis, Guild branches should feel monitored.

A Guildhall in Erin Elios might have:

- Required Order liaison
    
- Restricted job postings
    
- Mandatory magic incident reports
    
- Confession chamber nearby
    
- Informants among clerks
    
- A public reputation for “necessary but spiritually risky work”
    

Good tension:

> The Guild says: “Log the threat.”  
> The Order says: “Control the panic.”

Both have a point.

# Solanthis Encounter Style

Solanthis enemies should not fight like bandits.

They fight with doctrine.

## Crownwarden tactics

- Protect VIPs first.
    
- Form shield walls.
    
- Force enemies into bright open spaces.
    
- Use radiant zones to deny movement.
    
- Mark casters for suppression.
    
- Grapple or restrain rather than kill if public witnesses are present.
    

## Inquest tactics

- Disable, isolate, interrogate.
    
- Use silence, counterspell, nets, manacles, glyphs.
    
- Target spell components and holy symbols.
    
- Split the party socially before splitting them physically.
    

## Fanatic tactics

- Willingly die to complete rituals.
    
- Chant to coordinate timing.
    
- Use civilians as moral terrain, not necessarily hostages.
    
- Trigger “purity mechanisms” that hurt everyone, including themselves.
    

## Celestial monsters/constructs

Solanthis should have sacred guardians that feel beautiful and wrong:

- Sun-lacquered statues that bleed light
    
- Angelic masks mounted on empty armor
    
- Lion-like radiant constructs with human voices
    
- Mirror-seraphs that duplicate spellcasters
    
- Bell-wraiths made from failed confessions
    
- Blindfolded execution saints

# The Best Version of Solanthis

Solanthis should make the players ask:

> “Are we fighting the people preventing the apocalypse?”

That is the gold.

Because the Order is not wrong that magic is dangerous.  
They are not wrong that the Grey could return.  
They are not wrong that panic can kill nations.

But they are wrong to believe truth belongs only to them.

That should be the soul of Solanthis.
