### Faction Reputation Has Teeth — "Let the Numbers Mean Something Daily"

**The problem it solves:** You are already tracking reputation as a number. Without mechanical teeth, players will treat it as a flavour tracker rather than a resource to manage.

**The rule — Reputation Thresholds:**

|Reputation|What changes in daily life|
|---|---|
|+3|One named faction contact will do one significant favour per session, no questions asked. The party is recognised and welcomed without rolls in faction-controlled spaces.|
|+2|Faction merchants give 10% discount. Faction guards wave them through. One free piece of intelligence per session from a faction contact.|
|+1|Faction NPCs start at Friendly rather than Neutral. Social checks with faction-affiliated people have advantage.|
|0|Normal. No modifier.|
|−1|Faction NPCs start at Suspicious. Social checks with faction-affiliated people have disadvantage. They are quietly watched.|
|−2|The faction actively creates one obstacle per session — a tail, a blocked road, a competitor hired to undercut them, a contact who suddenly becomes unavailable.|
|−3|Openly hunted. Faction guards have standing orders. Entering faction-controlled territory requires disguise or significant prep.|

**The Celestial Order** gets its own reputation track separate from the three nations, since it operates across borders.

**Why it works at your table:** It makes every interaction with a faction-affiliated NPC carry context. It also means the party's choices about who to help, what information to share, and who to deceive have daily consequences they can feel, not just long-term narrative ones.
